How to get the sideways and upward speeds from a launch

In lesson 1 you used two speeds: the sideways speed and the upward speed. But when you throw something, you usually know one speed and an angle instead, like "20 m/s at 35°". This lesson shows you how to turn that into the two speeds your program needs. It takes one right triangle and two lines of trigonometry.

The launch speed as a right triangle

Draw the launch speed as an arrow at the launch angle. From the tip of the arrow, draw a line straight down to the ground. You now have a right triangle. The arrow is its longest side, called the hypotenuse. The side along the ground is the sideways speed, vx. The vertical side is the upward speed, vy [OpenStax University Physics, 4.3].

Now the trigonometry. Cosine gives the side next to the angle, so it gives the sideways speed. Sine gives the side opposite the angle, so it gives the upward speed. Keep the velocity components sheet open if it helps.

vx = v · cos θ

vy = v · sin θ

Why sine gives the upward speed

The angle sits at the ground. The side opposite that corner is the vertical side, and sine is “opposite over hypotenuse”. Quick check: at 90° the throw is straight up, and sin 90° = 1.
