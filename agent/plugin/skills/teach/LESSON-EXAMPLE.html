Split the velocity

A launch gives you one speed and one angle. The two rules from lesson 1 need two speeds: one sideways, one upward. Trigonometry converts the first form into the second.

Why this matters for your mission. vx = v·cos(θ) and vy = v·sin(θ) are the first two lines of every trajectory program.

The split

Draw the launch speed as an arrow at the launch angle. Drop a line straight down from its tip to the ground. You now have a right triangle: the arrow is the hypotenuse, the ground side is the sideways speed, and the vertical side is the upward speed [OpenStax University Physics, 4.3].

Cosine gives the side next to the angle, and sine gives the side opposite it:

vx = v · cos θ

vy = v · sin θ

Why sine is the vertical one

The angle sits at the ground. The side opposite that corner is the vertical side, and sine is “opposite over hypotenuse”. Quick check: at 90° the throw is straight up, and sin 90° = 1.
